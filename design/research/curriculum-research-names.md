# Letter names: when and how they come in (both languages)

Nov 25, 2026 · agent `curriculum-research-names`
(`tasks/pending/curriculum-research-names.md`)

The research behind the **proposed** letter-names section of
`docs/curriculum.md` (§9, marked "proposed, for the human to agree"). It
answers the brief's five questions:

1. what the schools do;
2. where the two languages' names and sounds clash;
3. the design;
4. the voice lines;
5. the questions for the human.

It also answers `handwriting`'s open question: should „Bravo, ai făcut litera
{x}!” say the letter's sound or its name, and when does that change?

Every claim about how a school teaches, or about what research found, has a
label: [R…] for Romanian sources, [E…] for English ones and [B…] for
research papers. The labels R1–R9, E1–E4 and B1–B16 are the same sources as
in [`curriculum-research.md`](curriculum-research.md) §1; the new ones are
listed in §1 below. Where a source could not be read, it says so.

**The short answer** (for the human, §7):

- **When:** after planet 6, at an **Alphabet Station** ("Stația
  Alfabetului") between planets 6 and 7, in both languages, Romanian first.
  This is the stage where English phonics teaches names. It also comes just
  before planet 7's English digraphs (sh, ch, th), which are taught by naming
  their letters (§2.2).
- **How:**
  - a short song at the start of each names lesson;
  - a "name round" for each letter she knows, with the name and the sound
    always said together;
  - a game where she hears a name and taps the letter;
  - a planned bridge, at the start of each English names lesson, that puts
    the two languages' names side by side.
- **New and reused activities:** one new activity kind, `SingAlong` (a song
  whose items light up in order, then "what comes next"). The other parts
  reuse `MeetLetter`, `ListenAndPick` and `TwoSounds`.
- **Romanian names:**
  - consonants use the forms the Academy's DOOM 3 allows that start with the
    letter's sound: **be, ce, de, fe, ge, le, me, ne, pe, re, se, te, ve,
    ze**;
  - never em, el, en, es, ef, which are the English names;
  - never mî, bî, which are how DOOM 3 cites the sounds.
- **Spliced lines:**
  - a line that *mentions* a letter („Bravo, ai făcut litera {x}!”) says its
    name once she has met that letter's name, and its sound until then;
  - a line that *asks* for a letter („Atinge litera {x}.”) keeps the step's
    cue (the sound, unless the step is about names);
  - a line about a sound („Ce sunet auzi după {x}?”) always says the sound.

## 2. What the schools do

### 2.1 Romanian

- **Every lesson is "the sound and the letter".** The lesson titles read
  "Sunetul și litera m" and "Sunetul și literele a și A" ([R1] contents,
  [R4], [R5]). The method is phonetic, analytic and synthetic: a sound is
  heard in a word, then its letter is shown ([R6] 1.3: "analiza și sinteza
  fonetică a cuvintelor"). Nothing in the clasa I manuals read asks
  children to say a letter's name during its lesson.
- **The alphabet comes at the end of clasa I,** after all the letters:
  - in [R2], unit 9, "Casa alfabetului": "Alfabetul (1)" and "Alfabetul
    (2)", pp. 122–123, and "Știu întregul alfabet de la A până la Z",
    p. 124;
  - in [R1], "Alfabetul limbii române", p. 126 (a picture alphabet), and
    the same lesson in its planning [R12] ("9. Alfabetul limbii române");
  - in [R3], unit 10;
  - in [R11], the year's last revision includes "repetarea tuturor
    literelor învățate, în ordine alfabetică".
- **What the alphabet lesson asks for** ([R2] pp. 122–123):
  - "Spune, pe rând, literele din fiecare încăpere" (say the letters in
    turn);
  - "Citește și rostește literele care lipsesc" (read and say the missing
    letters);
  - "Spune vecinii fiecăreia" (say each letter's neighbours);
  - "Transcrie-le … ordonate alfabetic" (write them in alphabetical
    order);
  - "Numerotează cuvintele în ordine alfabetică" (number the words in
    alphabetical order).
  So in clasa I the order is taught too, by saying and by sorting.
- **The programme** [R6]:
  - for clasa pregătitoare (age 6), "recunoașterea și numirea literelor
    mari și mici de tipar" and "asocierea unor litere și sunete" (3.1);
  - for clasa I, "Alfabetul limbii române" among the contents, in both
    reading and writing;
  - for clasa a II-a, "ordonarea alfabetică a unor cuvinte date" (1.3).
- **No manual read prints the names.** [R1] p. 126 and [R2] pp. 122–123
  show the letters, not how to say them. The norm for the names is the
  Academy's dictionary, DOOM 3 [R10]. It gives:

  used: it is neither the pure sound nor a name distinct from it. DOOM 3

  always was, but all four are "încă percepută ca literă străină" [R10].
  The older norm for q was "chiu" (DEX '96, "[Pr.: chiu]"; DEX '09 has
  "kü") [R9].

### 2.2 English

- **Letters and Sounds** [E1] teaches the names in **Phase 3**, after Phase
  2's sounds (s a t p · i n m d · g o c k · ck e u r · h b f ff l ll ss):
  "They will learn letter names during this phase" (p. 74).
  - **Its timetable** (pp. 76–78): "Learn an alphabet song" in week 1, then
    "Point to the letters in the alphabet while singing the alphabet song"
    each week, and "Practise letter names" in the last week.
  - **"Teaching letter names (if not already taught)"** (p. 80), over two or
    three weeks:
    - "Teach the alphabet song and sing it every day for a week";
    - "Display a letter (e.g. t) and tell the children that it is a t (say
      its name) and stands for the sound /t/ (say its sound)";
    - "Display the alphabet frieze and point to the letters as the children
      sing";
    - "Pick out a few letters each day and connect the names with the
      sounds of the letter".
  - **Why names come before digraphs:** "When referring to the individual
    letters in a grapheme, the children should now be encouraged to use
    letter names as letters do not stand for their Phase Two sounds when
    they form part of two-letter and three-letter graphemes" (Phase 3,
    p. 84). That is exactly what Școlița's planet 7 (English sh, ch, th, ck,
    ng) needs.
  - **The order:** "By Phase Six, the repeated singing of an alphabet song
    at earlier phases should have familiarised them with alphabetical
    order", for dictionary work (p. 186).
- **The national curriculum** [E4], Year 1 (age 5–6), statutory: "name the
  letters of the alphabet: naming the letters of the alphabet in order;
  using letter names to distinguish between alternative spellings of the
  same sound". Read Write Inc.'s Year 1 map quotes the same requirement
  [E7].
- **The reading framework** [E5] (pp. 48–49):
  - "each lower-case letter has a corresponding capital letter; they share
    the letter name and represent the same sound";
  - "Some programmes teach the names of letters only once pupils have
    learnt to say the sounds".
- **Read Write Inc.** (OUP, a validated programme) tells parents: "Make sure
  they say sounds like 'mmm', not letter names like 'em'" [E6]. So the
  names stay out of the first phonics lessons.
- **Little Wandle** [E2]: its progression has no row for letter names (not
  found; §1).
- **What English children taught sounds first know** [B17]:
  - in England, adults "begin with sounds", and letter names are not
    formally taught until "the end of year 1"; in the US, adults stress
    names first;
  - English 5- to 7-year-olds were better at sounds and US children at
    names, and "differences between children in the two countries declined
    with age";
  - the authors found "no reason to believe that learning one type of
    label before the other" gives a lasting advantage: "children learn what
    they are taught".

### 2.3 What both systems share

- **Sounds first, names after** [E1] [E5] [E6] [R1] [R2]. v1 already
  agreed this.
- **Names with the whole alphabet, and its order:**
  - Romanian school: at the end of clasa I [R2] [R1] [R3];
  - English: from Phase 3 in Reception, with the song, the order by Year 1
    [E1] [E4].
- **A name is always tied to its sound** when taught: "it is a t … and
  stands for the sound /t/" [E1]. Teaching names and sounds together helped
  preschoolers learn the sounds more than teaching sounds alone [B22].
- **Names are for talking about letters,** not a reading skill in
  themselves. A meta-analysis found "minimal evidence of transfer of
  alphabet instruction to early phonological, reading, or spelling skills"
  [B21]. So names get a short unit and then everyday use, not a long
  track.

## 3. The clashes between the two languages' names and sounds

### 3.1 What the research says

- **Children learn the second label through the first.** Names that contain
  the letter's sound help children learn the sound, and names that do not
  contain it do not help [B18] [B19]. The sound's place in the name
  matters too:
  - in English, whether it is at the beginning or the end [B19];
  - in Brazilian Portuguese, only names that *begin* with the sound helped
    ("be", not "eli") [B20].
- **Errors follow the first labels learned** [B17]:
  - English children asked for names gave sound-based answers ("/kwi/ for
    the name of q");
  - US children asked for sounds gave name-based ones (/s/ for c, /dʒ/ for
    g, the "long" vowel sounds for the vowels).
  For Școlița, the sounds come first, so her errors will tend to be
  sounds given for names (as the English children's), not names for
  sounds.
- **Across two languages:**
  - Spanish-speaking kindergartners who knew Spanish letter names and
    sounds tended to know the English ones too, and the two "are fairly
    highly positively correlated across languages" [B23];
  - children carry their first language's letter–sound rules into the
    second's spelling in predictable ways [B15];
  - young bilinguals keep their languages apart [B16];
  - planned "bridge" moments, where the two languages are put side by side
    on purpose, are how biliteracy programmes handle the differences
    [B10].
  No study of Romanian–English letter names was found (§1), so the
  handling below is reasoned from these, not tested.

- **How a name is shown to be a name** (not a sound): design screen 2
  already draws them apart (`design/screens.md` §2):
  - the **sound** is a filled blue pill with a speaker icon (`/m/`);
  - the **name** is an outlined pill with a speech-bubble icon („me”).
  This becomes the rule everywhere a letter is spoken about: the speaker
  icon when a sound is said, the speech-bubble icon when a name is.
  Name and sound are also always introduced together in one sentence
  („Numele literei este me. Sunetul ei este mmm.”).

  colour **and** its badge (the star for Romanian, the ring for English).
  The name rounds are inside one language's lesson. The two languages'
  names meet only in the names bridge (§4.4), where both badges are on
  screen, as v1's `TwoSounds` does for sounds.

## 4. The design

### 4.1 Which Romanian names

**Recommended:** the DOOM 3 [R10] forms that start with the letter's sound,
with an e after the consonant:

> a, ă, â din a, be, ce, de, e, fe, ge, haș, i, î din i, je, ca, le, me, ne,
> o, pe, kü, re, se, șe, te, țe, u, ve, dublu ve, ics, i grec, ze

Why these forms:

- **They are the norm.** Each one is in DOOM 3 [R10].
- **They don't use the English names.** The names em, el, en, es, ef and
  zet are DOOM 3 alternatives that are the English names (or close to
  them, for zet and "zed").
- **The sound comes first.** Names that start with the letter's sound are
  the kind that help link the two [B18] [B19] [B20].
- **They are already used.** They are the names already in
  `content/voice-lines.csv` for planets 1–3 (me, te, pe, se, ne, de).
- **They avoid "mî".** DOOM 3 [R10] cites the *sound* with î ("mî"), so a
  "mî" name would blur the name and the sound.

Open choices (questions 2–4):

- "ge" or "ghe" for g;
- "kü" or "chiu" for q;
- whether her school uses other forms (if her teacher says "em" or "mî",
  the human may prefer to match).

### 4.2 When

- **After planet 6, before planet 7** (v1's plan, now with its reasons):
  - **The stage.** By then she knows 22 letters' sounds, a little more
    than Letters and Sounds' Phase 2 (about 23 graphemes) when it teaches
    names [E1].
  - **Planet 7 needs the names.** Planet 7 brings the English digraphs,
    which are taught by naming their letters [E1].
  - **So does planet 9.** Planet 9 brings the Romanian groups (ce, ci, che
    …), for which a name helps in the same way ("litera ce și litera e";
    "ce" is also the group).
  - **She is ahead of school, as for handwriting.** It comes well before
    the Romanian alphabet lesson, at the end of clasa I [R2]. Școlița is
    already ahead of clasa pregătitoare for handwriting (v1 §1).
- **Both languages, Romanian first,** as every planet (decision D1): for
  each group of letters, the Romanian names lesson, then the English one.
- **The names are not taught in planets 1–6.** v1 stands: letters are
  spoken of by their sound.

### 4.3 What teaches them (reusing the activity kinds)

**The Alphabet Station** ("Stația Alfabetului"), a stop on the map between
planets 6 and 7. It is not a planet: it has no new letter. It holds six
small names lessons per language, one for each planet's letters, so that
there are **a few names each time** [E1]:

| Lesson (RO, then EN) | Letters |
| --- | --- |
| names 1 | a m t p |
| names 2 | s i n |
| names 3 | o d e |
| names 4 | u r c l |
| names 5 | b f g h |
| names 6 | v z j k |

The station ends with a party in each language.

**A names lesson** (3–5 minutes):

1. **`SingAlong` (new kind):** the language's alphabet song, with the
   alphabet's letters on a frieze:
   - the letters she knows are bright, and the others are faint (heard, not
     taught: "listen only");
   - each letter lights up as it is sung, and the lesson's letters glow;
   - she can tap any letter to hear its name.
   Letters and Sounds sings the song daily while pointing to the frieze
   [E1].
2. **`MeetLetter`, name round,** once per letter of the lesson:
   - the card shows the letter in both cases (the name is the same for a
     and A [E5]), with its **sound pill** and its **name pill**
     (design screen 2);
   - the mascot says „Numele literei este {xname}. Sunetul ei este
     {xsound}.”;
   - she taps both pills.
   For a Romanian vowel, the line „La litera aceasta, numele și sunetul
   sunt la fel.” follows, because DOOM 3 gives the vowels no separate name
   [R10]. Elsewhere it is „Ascultă: numele și sunetul sunt diferite.” This
   connects each name with its sound [E1] [B22].
3. **`ListenAndPick`, names cue:**
   - „Atinge litera care se numește {xname}.”;
   - 2–4 letters she knows, as planets;
   - the hint ladder: the name again, then the name with the sound ("The
     letter called em has the sound mmm"), then the answer shown.
4. **The checkpoint:** „Bravo, acum știi numele acestor litere!”

**Each English names lesson** starts with the **names bridge** (§4.4) for
its group's clashing letter. This mirrors v1, where the English letter
lesson starts with `TwoSounds`.

**The station party** (per language):

- `SingAlong` with the whole song, then its "what comes next" round
  (§4.5);
- a mixed `ListenAndPick` (names cue) over all 22 letters;
- the station's sticker (`rewards`).

**From planet 7 on**, a new letter's `MeetLetter` has the name round after
the sound:

- the order is hear, see, then name, so the sound still comes first;
- the name pill on design screen 2 comes back;
- the new letter's name is not in the station's lessons, because it is met
  with the letter.

`ListenAndPick` and `LetterHunt` in letter lessons **keep the sound as
their cue**: reading needs sound to letter, and names are only for talking
about letters [B21].

**Why one new kind.** AGENTS.md says a new domain adds a kind only when no
existing one fits. Here:

- `MeetLetter`, `ListenAndPick` and `TwoSounds` fit with a "cue" option
  (sound or name);
- an ordered song with a "what comes next" round has no kind yet.

`SingAlong` is made domain-agnostic: its items are any ordered sequence.
That covers the alphabet now, and later:

- numbers 1–20 (counting, `domain-numbers`);
- do-re-mi (a scale, `domain-music`).

The modes:

- **listen**: the song plays, and the items light up;
- **tap**: hear any item;
- **what comes next**: an item lights, and she taps the one after it among
  three. The hint is the song up to that item; on the second miss, the
  answer is shown.

### 4.4 The names bridge (`TwoSounds`, names cue)

`TwoSounds` is reused with names instead of sounds:

- one letter on two little planets, each with its badge;
- she taps each to hear its name (RO „e”, EN "ee");
- then she hears a name and taps the planet it belongs to. The sorted
  items are names, not pictures; the follow-up brief's Design note settles
  that change to `TwoSounds`.

Where the bridge comes:

| English names lesson | Bridge |
| --- | --- |
| names 1 | a, with the mirror shown on m |
| names 2 | i |
| names 3 | e, and a light one for o |
| names 4 | u |
| names 5 | g |
| names 6 | j |

The bridge makes the clash visible instead of hoping it isn't noticed,
as v1 does for the vowels' sounds [B10] [B15].

### 4.5 The alphabet's order

**Taught lightly, by ear:**

- each language's own song;
- from the station party on, the "what comes next" round of `SingAlong`,
  which is [R2]'s "Spune vecinii fiecăreia" (say each letter's neighbours).

Both school systems make the order a goal of the first school year, not
earlier:

- Romanian clasa I, sorting by order in clasa a II-a [R2] [R6];
- English Year 1 and the dictionary work of Phase 6 [E1] [E4].

So Școlița does not test it, beyond the round. The two orders differ (ă â
after a, î after i, ș after s, ț after t, in Romanian only), and each song
keeps its own language's order.

### 4.6 The songs

- **English:** the traditional alphabet song, British (ending in "zed",
  [E8]; British English is decision D4). Letters and Sounds asks for "an
  alphabet song" [E1].
- **Romanian:**
  - no song is printed in the manuals read, and none is in the programme
    [R1] [R2] [R6];
  - **proposed:** the 31 names chanted in rhythm on `Syllables`' drum,
    ending „Acum știu tot alfabetul!”. That ties into the drum she already
    knows, and the two songs sound different, which keeps the languages
    apart;
  - or a tune the human knows from her kindergarten or school (question
    6).
- The music, its recording and its loudness belong to `sound-feel` and
  the family's recordings (out of scope).

### 4.7 The spliced lines: from the sound to the name

Every `{x}` / `{y}` in `content/voice-lines.csv` belongs to one of three
kinds of slot. Each kind has one rule, owned by one place in Core:

| Kind of slot | Lines (ids without the language) | What is said |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A sound slot**: the line is about the sound | `line-meet-listen`, `line-build-hint-after` („Ce sunet auzi după {x}?”), FirstSound's hints, the slow and stretched words, `TwoSounds`' sound lines | **Always the sound,** in every planet |
| **A cue slot**: the line asks her to find the letter | `line-pick-letter-x`, `line-pick-hint-show`, `line-hunt`, `line-hunt-hint-twinkle`, `line-hunt-hint-more` | **The step's cue:** the sound, unless the step is a names step (`cue=name`), which uses its own lines (§5.2) |
| **A mention slot**: the line refers to the letter | `line-today-letter`, `line-trace-done`, `line-trace-done-hand`, `line-word-join` („Bravo, ai făcut litera {x}! Acum leag-o de litera {y} …”) | **The sound until she has met that letter's name** in that language (the station's name round, or the letter's own `MeetLetter` from planet 7); **the name after** |

This answers `handwriting`'s question:

- „Bravo, ai făcut litera {x}!” keeps the sound through planet 6;
- after the station it says „litera me”, „litera be”;
- for a planet-7 letter, it says the sound until her first `MeetLetter` of
  it, then the name.

**In Romanian the switch changes only the consonants:** the vowels' names
are their sounds [R10].

**Every combination stays correct:**

- the names are invariable nouns in apposition to „litera” („litera me”,
  „litera î din a”, „litera dublu ve”);
- „leag-o” still refers to „litera” (feminine);
- English "the letter em" is fine for every name.

**The bubble** shows the letter itself in both cases, with the speaker or
the speech-bubble icon (§3.2).

**Rejected options:**

- **One switch for the whole language:** it would name letters she has not
  met by name.
- **Saying both** („litera me, mmm”): the lines get long, and the bubble no
  longer matches what is said.
- **A switch in the parent area:** one more setting, with no gain for her.

### 4.8 For other domains

The same three rules serve numbers and music:

- a numeral has a name ("șapte" / "seven") and a quantity;
- a note has a name ("do") and a pitch.

So a slot is a sound, cue or mention slot; the item kind decides what
"sound" and "name" mean.

### 5.3 Checked against AGENTS.md → "Romanian, checked"

- **No cedilla.** ș and ț are written with the comma below; checked by
  script on the CSV and this note: no U+015F or U+0163.
- **Every line is a full sentence with its verb.** The song is a list of
  names, which is what a song is, and it ends with a full sentence.
- **Nothing agrees with the child's gender:** „știi”, „ai cântat”, „Cântă
  și tu”, „Acum știu tot alfabetul!” (first person, the same for a girl or
  a boy). There is no child's name in any line.
- **Agreement with the word:**
  - „litera” is feminine: „Sunetul ei”, „Caut-o!”, „Atinge-o!”, „să-i
    auzi numele” (dative of „litera”), „Uite, după {xname} vine {yname}.
    Atinge-o!” (the letter);
  - „numele” is neuter: „ca să-l auzi”, „planeta lui”;
  - „sunetul” is neuter: „ca să-l auzi”;
  - „planeta” is feminine: „Atinge-o!”.
- **The names are invariable** in apposition („litera care se numește
  be”), so every spliced combination is correct (§4.7).
- **Diacritics:** „Stația”, „își”, „știi”, „Cântă”, „cântecul”, „până”,
  „după”, „Aceeași” (â inside a word, î at the start).
- **Hyphens:** „să-l”, „să-i”, „Caut-o”, „Atinge-o”. Punctuation: no
  space before „!” or „?”, and Romanian quotes in the notes.
